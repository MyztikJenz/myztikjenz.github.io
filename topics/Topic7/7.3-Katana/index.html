<  Katana

My oldest created a cardboard sword/katana many moons ago. They were really into making stuff to beat their sibling up with and cardboard was mostly bloodless.

This one was one of their better creations (the Needle from Hollow Knight was another).

I needed to get much better at Fusion before I could try to make this better and after the Pac-Man Battle Royale project, I had learned enough. And after a few YouTube videos, I had this:

Fusion didn’t like some of the things I asked it to do… the sweep from the hilt to the tip did OK, but it never quite got it right all the way through no matter how hard I tried. And the backside ended up square when the original was rounded. Fusion flat out refused to do this because the existing fillets and chamfers just got in the way.

I tried for about twelve seconds to modify it in Blender before I realized the fresh hell was I getting into.

Printed on a Prusa XL, which is the largest capacity printer I have access to, it needed to be printed in five parts. And it took 46 hours to print. It has an aluminum core (hollow pipes I got from the packaging for our couch years ago) so it’s pretty stable, but the glue lines aren’t the best. And it’s subtle in some of these pictures, but there was a layer shift that happened during printing. Super unfortunate.


The hilt is friggin’ amazing though. It has the same layer shift but you can’t tell and the wood/fabric effect turned out super nice. Used the slicer’s “add texture” feature and it did exactly what I hoped for.

I did notice only way too late… looking at these pictures, actually, that I messed up. The cross hatches were supposed to be aligned to the sides of the blade and not the front/back. :headslap: Oh well… it still looks nice.

Eldest was impressed when I gave it to them but they asked “Did I know you were making this?”. Yes, yes you were. But it’s been like three years. Shit sometimes takes a long time.
